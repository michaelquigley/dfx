## Stage 2 review: exhausted allocations

Michael's stage 2 review reproduced two allocation gaps: a dash its neighbors left negative height reached `BeginChildStrV` as a fill-relative size, and a 20×20 Faceplate grid cell drew its component at `{0,0}` once padding consumed it. `DrawDash` now resolves its panel rectangle (`panelRect`) before queueing `SetNextWindowPos`/`Size` or pushing rounding. That order is load-bearing: skipping the child after queueing would leak geometry into the next window. A skipped dash also clears `Focused`, or `DashManager` would route actions to an invisible panel. Containers measure after `BeginChild` through `usableSize` and skip content when padding leaves nothing. Regressions: `TestAllocation_PaddingExhaustsContent` and `TestAllocation_DashExhaustedSide`, each mutation-checked against its guard. The review's separate TopMargin and mutable-theme-ownership findings are deliberately untouched, left for later triage. Terminus review `7b02fba99b5b`, covering this fix, was `clean`; its only advisories re-raise the vetoed lowercase-gallery and spacing-hierarchy Mermaid findings. The tree handed back is the reviewed tree.

The planning agent independently verified the exhausted-allocation fix: both original overlay reproductions, `make test`, the full build, and diff checks pass. That finding is resolved.

## Stage 2 review: TopMargin

Michael approved treating `DashManager.TopMargin` as reserved space above the entire manager layout, under both precedence modes. Offset the origin and reduce available height once, then derive all panels and the inner surface within that remainder; merely moving the side panels down preserves the reproduced overflow. Mutable-theme ownership remains a separate, untriaged finding.

Implemented. Offsetting the origin alone left top and bottom panels at their full `CurrentSize`: a remainder shorter than a dash let the bottom panel reach up into the inset, and an exhausted remainder still created the top panel. So `panelRect` now clamps every panel to its bounds, and the manager clamps `topHeight` to the remainder. That clamped value is also the top dash's `bounds.H`, which `dragHandlePos` uses to place the top handle; they must stay the same value. The `max(0, …)` on the reduced height is defensive only: the bounds and inner guards already skip negatives, so no mutation of it fails a test. Left and right widths are still not clamped to the manager's width. That is a horizontal-axis gap outside this finding, deliberately left alone. Regressions: `TestAllocation_DashManagerTopMargin`, `…TopMarginExhausts`, and `…TopMarginShortRemainder`, each mutation-checked against the guard it covers. Terminus review `4f4b52581723` was `clean`; its only advisories re-raise the vetoed lowercase-gallery and spacing-hierarchy Mermaid findings.

The planning agent independently verified the TopMargin fix, including the required panel clamping: the original TopMargin and earlier allocation reproductions, `make test`, the full build, and diff checks pass. That finding is resolved.

## Stage 2 review: theme ownership

`ownTheme` (style.go) copies only the built-in `*FaceplateTheme`, at startup after the effective theme is resolved and on every `CurrentStyle()` return. Hue schemes and `ModernDark` are deliberately not copied: they are shared package values or stateless, and a custom `Theme` keeps the application's ownership, as Michael directed (no generic cloning interface). A new built-in theme that carries configuration behind a pointer must be added to `ownTheme`, or it reintroduces this aliasing. `SetTheme` keeps no reference, so nothing else holds the active theme. Regressions: `TestFaceplateTheme_StartupOwnsItsCopy` (style and `Config.Theme` sources, shared font bytes) and `TestFaceplateTheme_SnapshotsAreIndependent` (snapshot independence; custom theme identity preserved), with each copy mutation-checked.
